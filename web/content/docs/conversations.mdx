Each bot has one chat that keeps going. You send it work, it replies, and everything stays in the same place on every device. This page covers what you can do in a bot's chat. For chats with several bots, see [Group chats](/docs/groups).

## What the chat shows

The chat shows your messages, the bot's messages, approval cards, choice cards and short notices. A bot's message arrives complete; nothing streams in word by word. A bot can send several messages in one turn.

## Choice cards

When your bot needs you to pick a way forward, it can show a question with two to twelve numbered options. On **iPhone**, **Android** and **Desktop**, tap the option you want, or type your own answer in the last row, **Something else**. **Suggested** marks its recommendation; nothing is selected for you. In the **Terminal UI**, use F1–F12, or 1–9 for the first nine options while the bot waits on the question (on a card left open, 1–9 jump to a bot as usual), or type your own answer in the message box. Your choice appears as your message and the bot continues its current work.

You can also answer in the message box: the full option, its number, or anything else in your own words. If an option itself is a number, the exact option text takes priority. A message with files, a quote or a `/` command doesn't answer the card; it moves the bot on from the question. On an older app, the question and numbered options appear as text, answered the same way. If you haven't answered after four and a half minutes, the bot goes on without your answer and the card stays open: pick an option later, or reply in the message box, and your choice reaches the bot as your next message, quoting its question. Stopping the bot while it waits on the card, the bot asking a new question, or restarting its computer expires it. An answered or expired card cannot be answered again.

Some agents, such as Hermes, ask their own questions mid-reply. They show on the same card, and **Approve automatically** never answers them: only you do. Such a question has options to pick, **Yes** and **No**, or no options at all, when you answer in your own words in the **Something else** row or the message box. When it has options, the answer must be one of them, so the card has no **Something else** row: anything you write in the message box goes to the bot as your next message, and the bot goes on without an answer to its question. The card waits until you answer, with no time limit; stopping the bot or the end of its reply ends the question unanswered. A question with several fields, a number, or several options to tick at once can't be shown on a card, so the agent hears that you didn't answer it.

While the bot works, a line under the last message shows what it's doing and for how long. It reads **Typing…** while the bot writes you a message.

To see more, turn on **Show thinking details** in Settings, under **Chat**. It's off by default and only changes the device you set it on. With it on, the line ends in an arrow. Tap or click it to unfold this turn's steps right in the chat: its thinking, what it said along the way, the tools and MCP tools it called and its plan. Tap a tool to see its output or diff. A long turn scrolls inside the line, which keeps showing the newest step. On the terminal UI, turn it on from `Ctrl-K` (**Show thinking details**), then press `>` or click the line. Each new turn starts folded, and the steps leave with the line when the bot finishes.

Everything else the agent did (its narration, thinking, tool calls, diffs and plans) is in **Full conversation**:

- **iPhone:** touch and hold a bot message, then **Show what it did**.
- **Desktop:** hover a bot message and choose **More message actions**, then **Show what it did**, or right-click the message.
- **Terminal UI:** `t` opens the trace pane, `T` opens it full screen, and `o` shows the last turn's steps.

When a Claude Code bot hands part of its work to a subagent, the subagent's steps sit indented under the step that started it, here and in a task's thread. Once the subagent is done, that step also shows its type, the tokens it used and how long it took, such as **general-purpose · 42.3k tokens · 6 s**; one that runs in the background reads **in background** instead. Other agents don't say which subagent did what, so their steps stay in one list.

## Sending messages

Type in the box at the bottom and send.

- **Desktop:** Return sends. Shift-Return or Option/Alt-Return starts a new line.
- **iPhone:** tap the send button. With a hardware keyboard, Return starts a new line and Cmd-Return sends.
- **Terminal UI:** press `i` or Enter to start typing, Enter to send, and Shift-Enter, Alt-Enter or Ctrl+J for a new line.

Each chat keeps its own draft on each device, and the draft comes back when you return to the chat or reopen the app. Drafts aren't synced between devices.

**While the bot works**, the box reads "Queue a message for" followed by the bot's name. Messages you send then wait in a list just above the box, which scrolls once it gets long, and they reach the agent together as its next turn. Each waiting message has two buttons:

- **Send now** (the turning arrow) hands it to the bot right away. Claude, Codex and omp bots take it into the reply they're working on, which then answers it too; with other agents, that reply stops and the bot starts on your message. While the bot is busy with something else, such as a routine, a group or another bot's request, the message goes first in line.
- **Remove** (the trash can) takes it back: neither it nor its files reach the bot.

In the terminal, the waiting messages show above the input; press `esc`, then `S` to send the oldest one now or `D` to take back the newest.

**Schedule a message** (desktop): instead of sending now, pick a time for it. The clock button beside the send button opens a time picker — the time is yours, in your own timezone, at least 30 seconds out — and the message waits above the box with its fire time beside it. When the time comes, it goes out like one you typed then: behind whatever the bot is working on at that moment, not before it. Scheduled messages survive a restart of the computer or the app. Every waiting message — queued or scheduled — can be clicked to read in full, copied, edited, or given a time with the same clock (on a host that predates the feature, the clock, the editing and their row controls stay hidden); **Send now** and **Remove** work on both kinds, and clearing the time sends a scheduled one like a normal message. The composer's clock takes a draft without files (files send immediately), but a queued message that already carries files can still be given a time from its row; the clock needs the computer online, while a plain text send still queues offline.

**When the computer is offline**, the iPhone can still take a text message: it shows **Waiting for the computer to come online** and goes out when the computer is back. It waits in the Codync relay, encrypted for your computer, for up to 24 hours; after that it shows **Failed to send**. With no relay (a computer in no account), it waits on the phone instead and goes when the phone reaches the computer again. Tap the cancel button beside it to take it back. A message with files can't wait; its send button stays off until the computer is online.

If a message fails to send, it shows **Failed to send** with buttons to resend or delete it. On **iPhone** and **Desktop**, a message's files stay on the device only until the app closes. After that, resending it shows an error instead of sending the text without its files: delete it and attach them again.

On iPhone, sending clears the message box so you can type your next message. You can add text with dictation or switch to another chat's saved draft.

**From another app (iPhone and Android):** in any app's share sheet, choose **Codync** to send text, a link, pictures or files (up to 10, each up to 100 MB) to a bot. The **Send to a bot** sheet shows what you're sharing, a message box with the shared text or link already in it, and your bots. Pick one and tap send. **Recent** lists the last three bots you shared to, and the one you shared to last is already picked. On the iPhone the sheet uploads the files, sends, and closes, back in the app you shared from. On Android, Codync opens that chat, where the message shows like one you typed. Text alone can go while the computer is offline and waits for it like any message (from the iPhone's sheet, only through the Codync relay). Files need the computer online. What you had typed in that chat's message box stays as it was.

**From Shortcuts (iPhone):** Codync's **Send Text to Bot** action sends a message without typing it in the chat. Pick a **Bot** and fill in **Text**. **Open Chat** is off by default, so the message goes out in the background; turn it on to open the chat. The action finishes when the computer accepts the message, or reports it queued when the computer is offline. The iPhone has to be unlocked, also from Siri ("Send text with Codync"). Only the current account's visible bots are listed.

## Files

Use the **+** button at the bottom left of the message box.

- **iPhone:** **Photos**, **Files**, or **Paste** when the clipboard holds an image. Photos are sent as JPEG.
- **Desktop:** pick files, drop them on the box, or paste a copied image or file with Cmd+V (Ctrl+V on Windows and Linux).
- **Terminal UI:** drag a file onto the terminal to paste its path. Backspace in an empty box removes the last file.

A message can be files only. Each file can be up to 100 MB. The files are saved on the computer, in an `uploads` folder in the bot's own folder. The agent gets your message with the path of each file and reads them with its own tools.

Bots can send files back when you ask, such as a report or a build. Each one shows as a card with its name and size and a download action. An image up to 20 MB also shows the picture itself in the iPhone, Android and desktop apps; tap or click it to see it full size. On desktop, image previews fit the whole picture inside a larger panel that adjusts to your window. This also applies to images you send. In the full-size picture, the copy button puts the picture itself on the clipboard, and for a file a bot sent, the download button next to the close button saves it. You can zoom up to 8x:

- **Desktop:** pinch on the trackpad (or hold Ctrl and scroll) to zoom, drag to move the picture, and double-click to zoom in or back out. **+** and **-** zoom too, and **0** fits the picture again. Right-click a picture, in the chat or full size, for the message's usual menu, where **Copy** becomes **Copy image** and **Copy name**.
- **iPhone:** pinch to zoom, drag to move the picture, and double-tap to zoom in or back out. Touch and hold a picture, in the chat or full size, for the message's usual menu, where **Copy** becomes **Copy image** and **Copy name**.

A voice note or other sound file (MP3, M4A, WAV or AAC) up to 20 MB has a play button in place of its icon in the same apps. It spins while the sound loads; then the card shows the time played and its length over a progress bar. If the sound can't be played, the card says **Couldn't play** and the next tap tries again. With VoiceOver or TalkBack, each card is one item, with play and download among its actions.

To save a file:

- **Desktop:** saves through the system Save dialog.
- **iPhone:** opens the share sheet, including Save to Files.
- **Terminal UI:** pick the message (`v`), then `f` saves it to Downloads.

Codync keeps its own copy of each shared file, so it stays downloadable even if the bot later changes or deletes the original. Bots share files only in their own chat, not in group chats or routine runs.

A bot can also speak a message: its voice note then plays at the top of that message's bubble, with the text under it, instead of coming as a card of its own. The player works like a voice note's card: tap play, then the progress bar and the time played sit beside the button on one row; tap or drag the bar to jump to another point (on desktop, a click anywhere else on the player plays or pauses), and tap again if it says **Couldn't play**. To save the sound, pick Download in the message's menu (press and hold on the phones, right-click on desktop). The terminal UI shows the file's line with the text under it.

Playback errors use your app's language, with a tap-to-retry hint on phones and a
click-to-retry hint on desktop. On iPhone, playing a note after a call or dictation
uses normal playback audio rather than retaining the recording settings.
On Android, leaving the chat while a note is loading prevents it from starting afterward.

A report or a tracker you'll come back to can come as an [artifact](/docs/artifacts) instead: a page that opens from its card. Everything made or shared in a chat is also in its [Library](/docs/artifacts#the-library).

## Approval cards

When a bot's agent asks to run a command, change files or use another tool, an approval card appears in the chat, unless the bot's **Permissions** are **Approve automatically**. In the agent's Auto mode it asks only about what it won't decide on its own. It says what the agent wants ("Wants to run a command", "Wants to change files"…), the computer and folder it runs in, and **Details** with the command or the diff. In the desktop app an answered card folds to one line; click it to read it again.

The answers come from the agent, usually:

| Answer | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Allow once** | Allow this request. |
| **Always allow** | Allow it and requests like it, as the agent defines that. |
| **Deny** | Refuse this request. |
| **Never** | Refuse it and requests like it, as the agent defines that. |

In the terminal UI, press `y` to allow once, `a` to always allow, `n` to reject, `N` to reject and say why, or `1`…`9` to pick an option.

While a card waits, the bot's row in the bot list reads **Needs approval** and its unread badge counts the card in amber, even after you've opened the chat, until you answer it. A folded section with a waiting bot shows its badge in amber too.

The agent waits for your answer. If the turn ends before you answer, for example because you stopped it, the card reads **Expired — the agent moved on**.

With **Approve automatically**, no cards appear: Codync allows each request for you. A message from another computer that your network rules hold to **Auto** or **Ask** is the exception: what the bot does for it shows a card whatever this setting ([choose who reaches your bots](/docs/teamwork#choose-who-reaches-your-bots)). Your agent's own permission rules still apply under any setting. To change the setting, see [Bots](/docs/bots#permissions).

Some agents keep working after their reply, for example a background helper that finishes a long job. With **Approve automatically**, what that work asks is allowed too, unless you stopped the bot's latest reply; under any other setting, or after a message your network rules hold to Auto or Ask, it's declined, since no card can wait for you. Once the bot is idle there's no **Stop**: to stop approving that work, switch the bot to **Ask me**. When that work is done, the bot can still send you its result as a message in its chat, unless you stopped its latest reply.

A [thread](#work) asks in the same chat. Its card has the line **Work ·** and its task's title, and you answer it like any other card. A thread keeps the **Permissions** setting its bot had when it started. When a thread needs you to choose between ways forward, its choice card is in the chat too: pick an option on the card. It has no **Something else** row: a message you type goes to the bot in the chat and doesn't answer a thread's card, and **Stop** in the chat leaves it waiting; the card ends when you stop the task, or after four and a half minutes without an answer. The **Sign in** card of an agent a thread needs you to sign in to is in the chat too ([below](#work)).

## Work

A bot does long work (changing code, running builds or tests, research, writing a document) away from its chat. Each piece of that work is a **task**: it has a title, a status and, when it ends, a report. A **thread** is where the bot does tasks, started for them: like the chat, it has its own [session](/docs/bots#sessions), what the agent remembers there, and its own steps you can read. It does one task at a time and may go on to the next. A thread starts from what the bot wrote for the task and doesn't see the chat: if it missed something you said earlier, tell the bot, which passes it on. When a thread needs something only the bot can tell it, such as what you meant, it asks the bot and goes on with the answer; what only you can decide, the thread asks you on a card in the chat. A new task waits in the bot's **event box** until a thread takes it. The chat stays free, so you can keep writing to the bot and it keeps answering. You can give a bot several things: a set number of threads run at once, and the other tasks wait their turn in the event box.

**Start a thread yourself (desktop):** open **Threads**, click **+** at the top of the list (or, grouped by bot, the **+** beside a bot's name), type what it should work on and send; add files and pictures with **+**, as in a chat. Above the box you choose what the thread runs on: the **bot**, the **folder** it works in (in a bot's Git project, also **Here** or **New worktree**), its **permissions** and its **model** (with the model's own settings, such as effort), and in the model's menu the agent and [login profile](/docs/agents#login-profiles) it runs on. Each starts as the bot's own, and a change is for this thread only: the bot's settings stay as they are. The thread has the bot's agent, instructions, memory and connectors, but starts fresh, without the chat's conversation, and what it finds stays in the thread: nothing is posted in the bot's chat. It starts right away: it doesn't wait for a free place among the bot's threads (only while the agent is near its usage limit), and the bot doesn't see or steer it. It needs a computer on a Codync version that has this.

**A thread without a bot:** **New thread** starts with **No bot** (pick a bot in the bot menu above the box, grouped by computer when you have several). Choose the **computer** it runs on, then the **agent** (Claude Code, Codex, …) on one of its login profiles, the folder, permissions and model. The folder's first choice, **Own folder**, is a [folder of its own](/docs/working-folders#a-thread-without-a-bot) that Codync makes for the thread; the thread then shows **Own folder** where it says where it works, and grouped by folder, all such threads sit together under one **Own folder** group. The agent works on its own, as if you ran it yourself: none of a bot's instructions, memory or connectors, and none of Codync's tools. Its approvals show in the thread. Grouped by bot, these threads are under **No bot**.

- **The capsule.** While anything is being worked on or waits, a capsule above the message box reads **Work ·** and what goes on. With one task being worked on and nothing waiting, that is what its thread is doing and for how long. Otherwise it counts, such as **2 working · 3 waiting**, with **Needs you** first while a task waits on you: an approval or choice card, or an agent's **Sign in** card. The bot's row in the bot list shows the same counts while the chat itself is idle. Tap or click the capsule to open the bot's **Details**.
- **Details** lists the work, and leaves out a section that has nothing:
  - **Threads:** the tasks being worked on now, one thread each. The heading reads, for example, **2 / 3**: how many threads run, of the most the bot may run at once. One not in use reads **Free**. A thread you started yourself doesn't count. A thread that waits for you (an approval, a choice or a sign-in) doesn't count, so the next task can start meanwhile; when you answer, it goes on, even if that makes one more than the number.
  - **Event box:** the tasks that wait, numbered in the order they will be taken, each with why it waits: **Next up**, how many are ahead, **Follows what it works on** or **Near the usage limit: one at a time**. A routine's run that waits for its routine's previous run also shows **Next up**, and the tasks behind it may start before it.
  - **Recent:** the tasks that ended in the last day.
- **To see what a thread did,** tap or click its task's row under **Threads** or **Recent**. The thread opens in place of the list, not in a window of its own:
  - **Desktop:** the thread opens as a tab of the details panel, named after its task, next to Details and the other tabs, and reads like the bot's chat, as on the **Threads** page (below). Close its tab when you are done.
  - **iPhone and Android:** the thread opens as a page inside the details sheet, in the same form as on the desktop. **Back** at the top left, or swiping back from the left edge (on Android, the system back), returns to the list.
  - **Terminal UI:** Enter on the row opens the thread's steps over **Details**; Esc closes them.

  On iPhone and Android the thread reads like the bot's chat, as on the desktop. At the top: **Back**, the bot (a thread without a bot: its agent), the task's title, and under it how it stands and for how long; **Details** at the top right opens the bot's details (not for a thread without a bot, nor when the thread is already inside them). Below, **What it was told** unfolds to what the thread started with; then your messages to it and its replies as the chat's bubbles, which you can copy. What the thread puts in the chat (an approval or a choice it asks of you, its agent's **Sign in** card, a file, a voice note, an artifact) shows right there, as in the chat: answer the card, download the file or open the artifact in the thread. While the thread works, the chat's working line shows what it is doing and for how long; with thinking details on (Settings), tap it for its steps. The message box shows the agent it runs on and, unless it works in the bot's own folder, where it works: the folder, or the branch when it has a worktree; tap that to copy the full path. A row under **Threads** or **Recent** that needs you opens the chat at its card.

  Terminal UI: the top of the thread has the task's title, how it stands and for how long, **Stop** while it's being worked on, and where the thread works. Below it, **What it was told**, its steps, your messages and its replies, and what it put in the chat, shown as in the chat: an approval or choice card whole, a file or an artifact as the bot's message. Answer a waiting card right there with the chat's keys: `y`, `a` or `n` on an approval (`N` denies and lets you tell the thread why), F1–F12 on a choice, or 1–9 on either; `C` opens a waiting **Sign in**. Tab picks the newest of them and goes up, Shift-Tab down; on a picked file or artifact `o` opens it in your browser and `f` saves it, and Enter goes to the chat at it.
- **To write to a thread,** type in the box under it, **Message this thread**, as in a chat. This works for the threads of the bot's own tasks. While the task is being worked on, your message waits its turn, and **Send now** and **Remove** work as in the chat: on the desktop they are under the waiting message, which reads **Queued**; on iPhone and Android the message waits in the list just above the box. Sending it now stops what the thread is doing for your message when its agent can't take a message mid-turn. The thread of a task that already ended takes a message too: the task goes back under **Threads** and is worked on again, in the same session where the agent can pick it up, even when every thread is busy. When it ends, the bot tells you in the chat again, and knows what you wrote to the thread. A message never answers a card the thread waits on; **Stop** drops the messages still waiting. A routine's run and another bot's request can be read and stopped, not written to: one line shows instead of the message box, **Read only: a routine's run** or **Read only: another bot's request**. Photos and files go with a message as in the chat: the **+** in the box, or drop or paste them on it; the thread's agent reads them from your computer. On iPhone, Android and the terminal UI a thread takes no voice or commands; on the desktop its box is the chat's, with dictation but not its clock (a thread's messages are never scheduled). On the desktop, iPhone and Android, while the thread works the box's stop button (or, on a read-only thread, the **Stop** beside its line) stops it. Terminal UI: in a thread's steps, press `i` or Enter to type, Enter to send and Esc to stop typing; while typing, drag a file onto the terminal to attach it, and Backspace in an empty box drops the last one; then `S` sends the next waiting message now and `D` removes the last.
- **Threads** shows every bot's work in one place. On iPhone and Android it's a tab of its own, between **Bots** and **Workflows**, its badge the number of tasks being worked on, and it shows the threads of the computers the Bots tab shows. It lists them like the desktop's (below): **Needs you** first, then groups, then **Recent**, newest first (the first 8, then **Show more**), each thread one line with the icon of the coding agent it runs on (after it falls back, the one it switched to) and a mark for how it stands. The filter button at the top right groups them **by folder** (the default), **by bot** or **by computer**, and can sort them **by status** or **by last active**; this phone keeps the choice. Tap a group's name to fold it. Tap a thread to open it full screen, in the same form as on the desktop (above), the thread scrolling under **Back** and **Details** at the top; **Back** returns to the list. A thread that needs you shows its card there to answer. Touch and hold a thread for its menu: **Open thread**, **Open chat** (**Answer in chat** when it needs you), **Stop**, **First**, **Cut in**, **Try again**, **Copy title**, **Copy folder path** and **Drop**, each where it applies. The search button beside it finds a thread by its title or its bot's name; **Cancel** goes back to the list. **+** opens a new thread full screen, as on the desktop: a chat nothing was said in yet, starting with **No bot** where your computer can run one so, else with your lead bot. Write its first message (text only) and tap Send, and the page becomes the new thread's chat; **Back** returns to the list. If it can't start, why shows under the box and your text stays. In the box's row, the first chip picks who it's with (a bot, or **No bot**, the dashed square). With a bot it works in the bot's default project (its own folder when it has no projects), on the bot's model and approvals. Without one, the second chip picks the agent, login profile and model in the model panel; it works in a folder of its own and asks before what it does. Its folder, permissions and the computer it runs on are chosen on the desktop. With no tasks at all it reads **No threads right now**. Desktop: **Threads** (with the same number) in the bar of icons at the left of the window lists every bot's threads like a chat app's sidebar (**+** at the top starts one, and the search button finds one by title or bot): **Needs you** first, then groups, then **Recent**, each thread one line with the icon of the coding agent it runs on at its start and a mark at its end for how it stands (a pulse while it works, amber when it needs you, red when it failed). The filter button at the top groups the threads **by folder** (threads working outside their bot's own folder, and each routine's runs), **by bot** (in your bot list's order) or **by computer**; click a group's name to fold it. Click a thread to open it beside the list. Hover a thread and click **…** at its end, or right-click it, for its menu: open the thread or its bot's chat, start another thread with the bot, **Stop** it, move a waiting one **First** or let it **Cut in**, **Try again**, copy its title or folder path, and **Drop** one that isn't running. It reads like the bot's chat: your messages and the bot's replies as bubbles, an approval or choice card right there to answer, files and artifacts to open, and the same message box to write to it (text, dictation and files), with the coding agent it runs on and the folder it works in beside the microphone. When you start one with **+** and a bot, the folder chip lists the bot's projects first (the default picked), then **Own folder**, then folders you used recently, then **Browse other folders**. In a project in a Git repository you also choose where it works, with the branch button beside the folder: **Here**, in the project itself, or **New worktree**, a [worktree](/docs/working-folders#where-threads-work-in-a-project) of its own, whatever the bot's **Worktree mode** says. The top of the window shows the thread's name and status; the panel button there opens the bot's panel beside the thread, and while it is open the same **Create template**, **Maximize panel** and **Split panel** buttons as in a bot's chat sit before it (no **Maximize panel** in a window too narrow for the panel to sit beside the thread, where it covers the thread already): **+** adds any of its views (Details, Computer, Library, Memory, Settings, Browser), a **Terminal** in the thread's folder, and **Side chat**, the bot's own chat to write to while the thread runs. Terminal UI: press `E` in the bot list; Enter on a bot's name opens its **Details**, on a row that needs you the chat, and on another task's row its thread's steps.
- **First** moves a waiting task to the front of the event box.
- **Cut in** is on the first waiting task when every thread is busy, unless that task follows work that isn't finished or waits for usage. On a routine's run that waits for its routine's previous run, or a task whose worktree is still being tidied, it only moves the task to the front. Otherwise it asks which task gives way: that one's thread closes and the task starts again later, and the task that cut in starts in its place.
- **Drop** removes a waiting task, or an ended one from **Recent**.
- **Stop** is on a task under **Threads**, in its thread, and on the capsule while one task is being worked on. Codync asks **Stop this task?** first. What the thread already changed stays.
- **Try again** puts a stopped or failed task back in the event box, last.
- **Too many in a row:** a bot can add at most eight tasks in a row without a message from you. Then the chat shows a note with a **Continue** button (**F** in the terminal UI): it sends the bot "Continue" from you, so it can go on.
- **Routine runs and other bots' requests** are tasks too: a [routine](/docs/routines#how-a-run-works) that comes due and a request [another bot](/docs/teamwork) sends wait in the event box and are worked on away from the chat, like the bot's own work. The result doesn't come back as the bot's report: a routine's reply is posted in the chat as before, and a request's answer goes to the bot that asked. **Stop** cancels the run or the request, **Try again** on a routine's task runs the routine again, and a request can't be tried again (the other bot asks again). They never give way to a task that cuts in, so **Cut in** shows only when one of the bot's own tasks is being worked on. Before its title, a routine's row has the routine symbol and a request's row the avatar of the bot that asked (Terminal UI: `↻`, or that bot's name and `›`). A bot with [computer use](/docs/computer-use) or **Manage the team** on does these in its chat instead, one at a time.
- **Where the buttons are.** Desktop: move the pointer over a row. iPhone and Android: a row shows one button; touch and hold the row for the rest. Terminal UI: the work shows as a line over the message box. Press Esc to leave typing, then `B` to open Details. On a row, Enter opens its steps, `X` stops it (it asks first), `F` moves it first, `C` cuts in, `D` drops it and `A` tries again.
- **When a task finishes,** its report goes to the bot, which tells you what it found in an ordinary message, with the usual notification. If a task couldn't finish, the bot tells you why. A task you stopped sends nothing.
- **Work a task leaves running.** When a thread stops while something it started in the background still runs, such as a review or a CI run it watches, the task isn't over yet. It stays under **Threads**, and a message from Codync in the thread asks it to wait for that work and then report. If it stops again while the work still runs, the task fails with the reason **it ended its turn while … still ran**, followed by what it last said, and **Try again** starts it over. A server it leaves running for you, such as a dev server, doesn't hold the task, and neither does something that already ran before the task began. Codync can't see every kind of background work: work an agent runs inside itself, and on Windows any program left running. This is for agents that don't say what they run in the background; Claude Code does, as the next point says.
- **Claude Code's background work.** In a thread, Claude Code tells Codync what it runs in the background: a command, a workflow or a monitor. While any of it runs, a capsule above the thread's message box reads **Background ·** and what runs, such as the workflow's name and its current step, with the time; several read as their names. The task stays under **Threads** as being worked on, and the thread doesn't end. When the work finishes, Claude Code picks it up by itself: the thread shows it working again, and what it says last is the task's report. If you stop a piece of that work and nothing else runs, Codync asks the thread to report what it has. A server it leaves running for you, such as a dev server, doesn't hold the task. Stopping the task stops that work with it, and if Claude Code itself stops meanwhile, the task fails with the reason **its agent stopped while its background work still ran**. A subagent Claude Code runs in the background keeps the thread working until it is done, its steps indented under the step that started it.
- **When one of the bot's own tasks looks stuck** (it repeats one step, keeps failing, or shows nothing new for an hour), the bot checks on it, at most once an hour, and may pass it a nudge or stop it; it tells you in the chat when it does, and says nothing when the task is fine. When such a task's approval or choice card has waited 2 hours, you get one reminder.

You can also tell the bot in the chat: to do one thing first, to do it now, to change what a task should do, or to stop it. What you add about a task that is already being worked on, the bot passes on to it: its thread reads it at its next step, or once its current step ends, and the task's one report comes after that. A waiting task reads it when it starts. The bot can also ask a task that is being worked on a question, such as how far it got, and get the thread's answer without stopping it. It waits up to a minute; an answer that comes later reaches the bot by itself, and it tells you then.

**Threads at once**, under **Workspace** in the bot's **Settings**, is the most threads the bot runs at the same time: 1 to 6, and 3 unless you change it. Threads you start yourself don't count toward it and run beside the bot's own. Each bot has its own number; there is no limit for the computer as a whole. While the agent a new thread would start on is near its usage limit, a bot starts one thread at a time.

**Advisor mode**, in the bot's **Agent** card under its fallbacks, runs the bot's threads on an agent of their own: the chat plans and hands work on, and the threads do it on another agent, model or login profile, with fallbacks of their own (**Threads' agent** and **Threads' fallbacks** show once it's on). For example, a strong model in the chat and a faster or cheaper one for the work. Turning it on starts the threads on the bot's own agent, model and fallbacks; change them from there. A thread on another agent than the chat's starts from a summary of the chat instead of a copy of its session. A thread keeps the agent it started on; the change applies to threads that start after it. Routine runs and other bots' requests run on the threads' agent too. Terminal UI: the **Advisor mode** field, then **Threads' agent** (←/→ agent, space model, `c` login profile) and **Threads' fallbacks** (the same keys as **Fallbacks**).

Threads use the bot's [fallbacks](/docs/agent-fallbacks) too (in Advisor mode, their own): when a thread's agent runs out of usage or is signed out, the next agent in the list carries the task on. Whatever you need to do about it shows in the chat with the task's line: the agent's **Sign in** card, or **Add a fallback** when the task failed without one. When no agent can go on until you sign in, the task waits: its row reads, for example, **Needs Claude Code sign-in**, and tapping or clicking it opens the chat at the card. Once you've signed in, the task goes on where it was. The bot can add work while up to 20 tasks wait. A thread can ask you to choose, share a file and publish an [artifact](/docs/artifacts): each shows in the chat with the line **Work ·** and its task's title.

**Worktree mode**, also under **Workspace**, shows once one of the bot's [projects](/docs/bots#projects) is in a Git repository: **Off**, **Side by side** (the default) or **Always**. It decides when a thread in that project gets a Git worktree of its own, so threads don't change the same files, and with **Always** none of them touches your checkout: a separate checkout in the Codync folder on your computer (`worktrees`), on a new branch named `codync/` and its task's title, started from the commit the project is on. With **Side by side** the first thread works in the project itself, and one that starts while another thread works in the same repository gets a worktree. Changes in the project that aren't committed aren't in it, and neither is what Git ignores there, such as installed packages, unless the project [fills new worktrees](/docs/working-folders#filling-a-new-copy) (`.worktreeinclude` and **Shared folders**). The bot can choose a worktree, or the project itself, for each task, whatever the mode. When the thread ends, a worktree with no changes and no commits is removed with its branch. Otherwise both stay, and the bot tells you the branch and where it is; ask the bot to merge it when you want it. Once you've merged it, the next time Codync starts on the computer removes the worktree with its branch. While a thread works in a worktree, its branch shows under its task's row and at the top of the thread. Codync never merges, rebases or deletes a branch that has work on it. Threads in the bot's own folder never get one: they work there side by side, and Codync keeps [versions](/docs/working-folders#versions-of-a-bots-own-folder) of it, so you can ask the bot to bring back what one thread overwrote. Routine runs and other bots' requests never get one either. How to bring a kept branch in yourself, and what happens in a folder that isn't a Git repository, is in [Working folders and git](/docs/working-folders#without-git).

A thread can add to the bot's [memory](/docs/memory) but can't change or remove what's there; ask the bot in the chat for that.

Tasks run on your computer and pick up again after the host restarts. The bot can look up what a task was asked and what it found for 30 days, so you can ask it about earlier work.

### Old threads

Threads are kept until you delete them. **Threads** lists the newest ones first; scroll to the end to load older ones, which open and take a message like any thread. In the terminal UI they are under **Recent** on a bot's Details page.

To clean up on its own, each computer has **Delete old threads** (in the desktop app under **Settings → Computers**, on the iPhone and Android under that computer in **Computers**, in the terminal UI in the command palette): **Keep forever** (the default), or **After 7**, **30** or **90 days**. The days count from when a thread last finished, so writing to one starts them again. A deleted thread takes its steps and, for a thread without a bot, its own folder with it.

## Stopping a turn

- **Desktop and iPhone:** while the bot works and the box is empty, the send button turns into **Stop**.
- **Desktop menu bar or tray menu:** the bot's submenu has **Stop**. It also stops the bot's [work](#work), the waiting tasks included.
- **Terminal UI:** press Esc to leave typing, then `s`.

Stop ends the running turn and drops the messages still queued for it, which then read **Not sent — stopped**. The chat shows "Stopped." and no notification is sent. The bot's [work](#work) keeps going: it has its own **Stop**, in the bot's **Details**. Only **Stop** in the desktop menu and stopping the bot from a [voice call](/docs/voice) stop it too. A task the bot added in the turn you stopped is dropped.

## Replying to a message

You can reply to any of your messages or any message from the bot. The reply goes in the chat in time order and shows a quote of the message it answers at the top.

- **iPhone:** touch and hold the message, then **Reply**.
- **Desktop:** hover the message and click **Reply**, or right-click it.
- **Terminal UI:** press `r`, move to the message with `j` and `k`, then Enter or `r`. Esc cancels.

To reply to one part of a message, select that part first:

- **iPhone and Android:** touch and hold the message, choose **Select Text**, select the words, then **Reply** in the selection menu.
- **Desktop:** select the words in the message, then click **Reply** on that message.

The quote is then only what you selected, and the bot is told your reply is about that part.

While you reply, the box shows the quote above it and reads "Reply…". The bot gets the quoted text with your reply, so it knows what you mean even when that message is from an earlier session.

A bot can quote a message too, usually when its answer comes later, after other messages. The quote shows above its message, so you can tell which question it answers.

Tap or click a quote to scroll to the message it quotes. In the terminal, pick the message with `v` and press `q`. The message has to be loaded in the chat: scroll up first to load older ones.

## Reactions

- **iPhone:** touch and hold a message; the reactions are at the top of the menu.
- **Desktop:** hover a message and click **Add reaction**, or right-click it.
- **Terminal UI:** pick a message (`v`), then `1`…`6`.

The quick reactions are 👍 ❤️ 😂 🎉 👀 ✅. Tap your reaction again to take it back. In a bot's own chat, the bot isn't told about your reactions. Bots react now and then too, and their reactions show with their avatars.

## Finding text in a chat

On the desktop, press ⌘F (Ctrl+F on Windows and Linux) to search the open chat. Matches are highlighted as you type and the bar shows which one you're on. Enter or ⌘G goes to the next match, Shift+Enter or ⇧⌘G to the previous one, and Esc closes the bar.

On the iPhone and Android, tap the magnifying glass at the top right of a chat. The bar shows how many matches there are and which one you're on; the arrows (or the keyboard's search key) move between them, and the message holding the current match is highlighted. Close the bar with ✕.

In the terminal UI, press `/` or Ctrl+F in a chat. Type to search, Enter or ↓ goes to the next match, ↑ to the previous one, and Esc closes the bar.

Find searches the messages the chat has loaded, so scroll up (or use **Load earlier messages** on the desktop) to reach older ones.

Searching your bots moved to ⌘K (Ctrl+K). ⌘F still opens it when no chat is open.

## Commands

Type `/` at the start of a message to see the bot's commands:

- `/fast`: turns [Fast mode](/docs/agents#models-and-options) on or off, when the bot's agent offers it.
- `/servers` (terminal UI): opens the bot's [dev servers](/docs/dev-servers), while it has some.
- `/` followed by a skill's ID: tells the bot to read that skill first and follow it for this message. The bot's skills are listed after the built-in commands.
- The agent's own commands (Claude Code's, Codex's, OpenCode's and so on), under the agent's name and marked with its logo, come last, apart from Codync's own commands and skills (under **Codync**), with the agent's description of each. In the terminal UI each of them names the agent. Picking one fills in `/<command>`; add anything it needs and send it. The list follows the agent the bot runs on now, and shows up once that agent has started for the bot. A group chat doesn't list them.

A message that starts with one of the agent's commands goes to the agent exactly as you typed it, so the agent runs it. Any other `/command` goes to the agent unchanged too, so the agent's own commands keep working. In the terminal UI that includes `/servers` while the bot has no dev servers. New session isn't a command; it's in the bot's menu in the list. See [Bots](/docs/bots#sessions).

## Follow-ups

When something needs another look later (a build that's still running, a reply that hasn't arrived), a bot can set itself a follow-up instead of only saying it will check back. Ask in plain words:

> Check the deploy again in 20 minutes and tell me if it failed.

- A line in the chat confirms it, such as "Follow-up in 20 minutes: Check the deploy". If there's no such line, no follow-up was set.
- When it's due, a "Follow-up · Check the deploy" line appears and the bot takes one turn in the same chat, with everything said before. Its reply notifies you like any other. If there's nothing worth telling you yet, it stays silent, and it can set another follow-up to keep watching.
- A bot doesn't set a follow-up to wait for its own [work](#work): each task's report comes into the chat by itself when it ends, and Codync wakes the bot if one looks stuck. Ask it to check back at a set time and it will.
- A follow-up happens once. For something that repeats, ask for a [routine](/docs/routines).
- Follow-ups work in a bot's own chat. In a [group](/docs/groups), or while it answers [another bot](/docs/teamwork), the bot can't set one: it tells you it can't check back from there. Ask it in its own chat.
- Waiting follow-ups are listed under **Later** on the bot's **Details** tab, above its [routines](/docs/routines#find-a-bots-routines), each on a card with a dashed outline, a clock and the time it happens. The ✕ on a card cancels it (terminal UI: pick it and press `x`), and a "Follow-up cancelled" line appears in the chat. You can also ask the bot what's waiting or to cancel one.
- Stopping the bot also drops a follow-up that was due and waiting for its turn; ones set for later stay.

A follow-up can wait from 1 minute to 30 days, and a bot can have 10 waiting at once. A bot that sets a new follow-up each time one comes due stops after 25 in a row and asks you whether to keep watching.

Follow-ups run on your computer, so the host has to be running and the computer awake. While one is waiting, Codync keeps the computer from going to sleep when idle. One that came due while the computer was off or asleep happens as soon as it's back.

## Fresh conversations

When a conversation in a bot's own chat has grown long, Codync may start the agent over with a summary instead of carrying all of it. The chat then shows a card saying why: the conversation is long and has gone quiet, you've been away a while after several summaries, or summarizing no longer frees enough room. Your next message starts the fresh conversation.

**What it carries** unfolds to what the new conversation begins with: the latest summary of the conversation, your last four messages word for word, the bot's last reply and its open plan. The bot can still search the earlier chat. The chat, memory, settings and files stay as they are.

Before your next message, you can:

- **Edit what it carries** (the pencil): change the text in **Edit the handoff** and save.
- **Keep this conversation:** the bot carries on in the same conversation.
- **Terminal UI:** pick the card (`v`), then `o` to show what it carries, `e` to edit it, or `K` to keep the conversation.

Afterwards the card folds into one line that unfolds to what was carried.

Fresh conversations come only from a Claude Code or Codex bot whose [Summarize long chats](/docs/bots#summarize-long-chats) setting is something other than Auto, the default. Changing it takes back a card the new setting no longer calls for, and the conversation carries on.

## Notifications

Your iPhone gets a notification when a bot:

| Kind | When | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Response needed** | It's waiting for you, such as on an approval card. | **Review request** |
| **Task complete** | It finished. Sent once, after its last queued message is answered. The text is an excerpt of its final reply. | **Open conversation** |
| **Task failed** | It hit an error or stopped short. | **Open conversation** |

You don't get one when:

- the Codync app on your iPhone is open and connected to the computer,
- the bot's **Notifications** switch is off, or the bot is hidden,
- you stopped the turn yourself.

The Codync icon on the Home Screen can show how many unread messages your chats have, the same count the chats' badges add up to. It's off by default: turn on **Unread count on app icon** in Settings → Notifications (and keep **Badges** on for Codync in iOS Settings). Turned off, the icon shows no number. A chat you read on your computer lowers it the next time a notification arrives or you open the app. On Android, the launcher badge follows your notifications, and a chat's notification goes away once you've read the chat.

Routine runs notify you too. A group chat sends one "done" once its bots have finished answering your message.

If system notification permission is off, **Push notifications** in **Manage computers** shows **Turn on notifications in Settings** instead of a switch. Tap it to allow notifications, or open iOS Settings if you previously denied permission. Returning to Codync refreshes the status in both Settings and Computer management. Your saved preference for that computer stays unchanged.

To turn notifications off on the iPhone, tap the filter button at the top right of the Bots tab, choose **Manage computers**, and switch off **Push notifications** on the computer whose alerts you don't want; each computer has its own switch. Previews, sounds and Focus follow your iOS settings. The push relay and Apple see only a generic line ("Your task is complete. Open Codync to read the result.") plus routing IDs. The bot's name and the message text are encrypted to your iPhone's key and decrypted on the phone. See [Privacy](/docs/privacy).

### On the computer

The desktop app can show the same notifications as system notifications on your Mac, Linux or Windows computer. They're off by default, since your phone already gets them and turning both on alerts you twice. Turn them on in Settings → General → System → **Notifications**. The computer shows one for each computer the app is connected to, even while your phone app is open, but not for a chat you have open in a focused window. Clicking it opens that chat, or the workflow when the notification is from a workflow. The bot's **Notifications** switch and hiding a bot apply here too. On macOS, allow notifications for Codync in System Settings → Notifications if none appear.

### Live Activities

When you send a message from the iPhone, a Live Activity for that bot appears on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. It shows whether the bot is working, needs you, is done or failed, without the message text. On the Lock Screen, a progress line of four segments in the state's color fills as the turn moves: one while your message is sent or queued, two while the bot works, three when it needs you, four once it ends. While the bot has [work](#work) going or waiting, the Live Activity stays on working after the bot's reply, shows that it needs you when a thread waits on a card, and ends when that work is finished and reported. A [voice call](/docs/voice#start-a-call) with the bot shows on its Live Activity too, with what the bot is doing while you talk. Turn it off in the **State** tab under **Live Activity**; that also keeps calls off it. The **Live Activity** and **Dynamic Island** settings control the same switch: the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island turn on and off together.

## Usage limits

Codync reads plan limits on your computer, without starting a conversation or model turn. It shows the agents your bots run. The button beside the computer's name opens every agent that can report, each with a switch and a handle, so you can turn on one no bot uses, hide one, or change their order: [which agents, and how](/docs/agents#usage-limits). To see them:

- **iPhone and Android:** the **State** tab, then **Usage limits**. There's also a Usage widget.
- **Desktop:** the menu bar or tray menu, or **Usage** (the gauge) in the bar of icons at the left of the window.
- **Terminal UI:** press `U`, then `s` to choose and arrange.

Each limit reads as the share already used, such as **62% used**. Widgets and the small meters in the island and the terminal UI's status bar show just the number, which is also the share used.

## From a script

`codync-host send` messages a bot from a terminal or a script on the computer the host runs on:

```sh
codync-host send --bot Reviewer "Review the current diff without changing files."
codync-host send --bot Reviewer --wait --json --file request.txt
git diff | codync-host send --bot Reviewer --wait --file -
```

- `--bot` takes the bot's name (any letter case) or its ID. When two bots share a name, use the ID.
- The message is the words after the options, or a UTF-8 file with `--file`. `--file -` reads it from the input.
- Without `--wait`, the command prints the message's `id`, `seq` and `turn` once the host has it. With `--wait`, it waits until the bot is done and prints its reply. A reply in several messages comes joined. A turn that ends with no text, such as only a reaction, prints nothing. Group chats can't be waited on.
- `--json` prints one line of JSON instead: `botId`, `entryId`, `entrySeq`, `turn`, `status` (`completed`, `failed` or `timeout`), `stopReason`, `reply`, `replyId`, `notices`, `elapsedSeconds` and `waitedForApprovalSeconds`.
- `--reply-to <id>` quotes a message, like [Reply](#replying-to-a-message): pass the `entryId` or `replyId` of an earlier `--json` result.
- `--timeout <seconds>` (30 minutes by default) is how long `--wait` waits. Time waiting for an [approval](#approval-cards) doesn't count, and the command never approves anything. When the time runs out, the bot keeps working.

Notices and approvals the bot waits for go to the error output. The exit code says how it went:

| Code | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Done (without `--wait`: the host took the message) |
| 1 | The turn was stopped, hit a limit, was declined or failed |
| 2 | Wrong options, no bot or more than one with that name, or `--wait` on a group chat |
| 3 | The host isn't running or refused the message |
| 4 | `--wait` ran out of time |
