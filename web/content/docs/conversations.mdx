Each bot has one chat that keeps going. You send it work, it replies, and everything stays in the same place on every device. This page covers what you can do in a bot's chat. For chats with several bots, see [Group chats](/docs/groups).

## What the chat shows

The chat shows your messages, the bot's messages, approval cards, choice cards and short notices. A bot's message arrives complete; nothing streams in word by word. A bot can send several messages in one turn.

## Choice cards

When your bot needs you to pick a way forward, it can show a question with two to twelve buttons. On **iPhone**, **Android** and **Desktop**, tap the option you want. **Suggested** marks its recommendation; nothing is selected for you. In the **Terminal UI**, use F1–F12, or 1–9 for the first nine options. Your choice appears as your message and the bot continues its current work.

On an older app, the question and numbered options appear as text. Reply with the full option or its number. If an option itself is a number, the exact option text takes priority. Sending a different message moves the bot on from the question. A card waits up to four and a half minutes; stopping the bot or restarting its computer also expires it. An answered or expired card cannot be answered again.

While the bot works, a line under the last message shows what it's doing and for how long. It reads **Typing…** while the bot writes you a message.

To see more, turn on **Show thinking details** in Settings, under **Chat**. It's off by default and only changes the device you set it on. With it on, the line ends in an arrow. Tap or click it to unfold this turn's steps right in the chat: its thinking, what it said along the way, the tools and MCP tools it called and its plan. Tap a tool to see its output or diff. A long turn scrolls inside the line, which keeps showing the newest step. On the terminal UI, turn it on from `Ctrl-K` (**Show thinking details**), then press `>` or click the line. Each new turn starts folded, and the steps leave with the line when the bot finishes.

Everything else the agent did (its narration, thinking, tool calls, diffs and plans) is in **Full conversation**:

- **iPhone:** touch and hold a bot message, then **Show what it did**.
- **Desktop:** hover a bot message and choose **More message actions**, then **Show what it did**, or right-click the message.
- **Terminal UI:** `t` opens the trace pane, `T` opens it full screen, and `o` shows the last turn's steps.

## Sending messages

Type in the box at the bottom and send.

- **Desktop:** Return sends. Shift-Return or Option/Alt-Return starts a new line.
- **iPhone:** tap the send button. With a hardware keyboard, Return starts a new line and Cmd-Return sends.
- **Terminal UI:** press `i` or Enter to start typing, Enter to send, and Shift-Enter, Alt-Enter or Ctrl+J for a new line.

Each chat keeps its own draft on each device, and the draft comes back when you return to the chat or reopen the app. Drafts aren't synced between devices.

**While the bot works**, the box reads "Queue a message for" followed by the bot's name. Messages you send then wait in a list just above the box, which scrolls once it gets long, and they reach the agent together as its next turn. Each waiting message has two buttons:

- **Send now** (the turning arrow) hands it to the bot right away. Claude, Codex and omp bots take it into the reply they're working on, which then answers it too; with other agents, that reply stops and the bot starts on your message. While the bot is busy with something else, such as a routine, a group or another bot's request, the message goes first in line.
- **Remove** (the trash can) takes it back: neither it nor its files reach the bot.

In the terminal, the waiting messages show above the input; press `esc`, then `S` to send the oldest one now or `D` to take back the newest.

**When the computer is offline**, the iPhone can still take a text message: it shows **Waiting for the computer to come online** and goes out when the computer is back. It waits in the Codync relay, encrypted for your computer, for up to 24 hours; after that it shows **Failed to send**. With no relay (a computer in no account), it waits on the phone instead and goes when the phone reaches the computer again. Tap the cancel button beside it to take it back. A message with files can't wait; its send button stays off until the computer is online.

If a message fails to send, it shows **Failed to send** with buttons to resend or delete it. On **iPhone** and **Desktop**, a message's files stay on the device only until the app closes. After that, resending it shows an error instead of sending the text without its files: delete it and attach them again.

On iPhone, sending clears the message box so you can type your next message. You can add text with dictation or switch to another chat's saved draft.

**From another app (Android):** share text to Codync, pick a bot, and its chat opens with the text in the message box, after anything you had typed there. Nothing is sent until you send it.

**From Shortcuts (iPhone):** Codync's **Send Text to Bot** action sends a message without typing it in the chat. Pick a **Bot** and fill in **Text**. **Open Chat** is off by default, so the message goes out in the background; turn it on to open the chat. The action finishes when the computer accepts the message, or reports it queued when the computer is offline. The iPhone has to be unlocked, also from Siri ("Send text with Codync"). Only the current account's visible bots are listed.

## Files

Use the **+** button left of the message box.

- **iPhone:** **Photos**, **Files**, or **Paste** when the clipboard holds an image. Photos are sent as JPEG.
- **Desktop:** pick files, drop them on the box, or paste a copied image or file with Cmd+V (Ctrl+V on Windows and Linux).
- **Terminal UI:** drag a file onto the terminal to paste its path. Backspace in an empty box removes the last file.

A message can be files only. Each file can be up to 100 MB. The files are saved on the computer, in an `uploads` folder in the bot's personal workspace, or in Codync's data folder for a bot that works in a project folder. The agent gets your message with the path of each file and reads them with its own tools.

Bots can send files back when you ask, such as a report or a build. Each one shows as a card with its name and size and a download action. An image up to 20 MB also shows the picture itself in the iPhone, Android and desktop apps; tap or click it to see it full size. On desktop, image previews fit the whole picture inside a larger panel that adjusts to your window. This also applies to images you send. In the full-size picture, the copy button puts the picture itself on the clipboard, and for a file a bot sent, the download button next to the close button saves it. You can zoom up to 8x:

- **Desktop:** pinch on the trackpad (or hold Ctrl and scroll) to zoom, drag to move the picture, and double-click to zoom in or back out. **+** and **-** zoom too, and **0** fits the picture again. Right-click a picture, in the chat or full size, for the message's usual menu, where **Copy** becomes **Copy image** and **Copy name**.
- **iPhone:** pinch to zoom, drag to move the picture, and double-tap to zoom in or back out. Touch and hold a picture, in the chat or full size, for the message's usual menu, where **Copy** becomes **Copy image** and **Copy name**.

A voice note or other sound file (MP3, M4A, WAV or AAC) up to 20 MB has a play button in place of its icon in the same apps. It spins while the sound loads; then the card shows the time played and its length over a progress bar. If the sound can't be played, the card says **Couldn't play** and the next tap tries again. With VoiceOver or TalkBack, each card is one item, with play and download among its actions.

To save a file:

- **Desktop:** saves through the system Save dialog.
- **iPhone:** opens the share sheet, including Save to Files.
- **Terminal UI:** pick the message (`v`), then `f` saves it to Downloads.

Codync keeps its own copy of each shared file, so it stays downloadable even if the bot later changes or deletes the original. Bots share files only in their own chat, not in group chats or routine runs.

A bot can also speak a message: its voice note then plays at the top of that message's bubble, with the text under it, instead of coming as a card of its own. The player works like a voice note's card: tap play, then the progress bar and the time played sit beside the button on one row; tap or drag the bar to jump to another point (on desktop, a click anywhere else on the player plays or pauses), and tap again if it says **Couldn't play**. To save the sound, pick Download in the message's menu (press and hold on the phones, right-click on desktop). The terminal UI shows the file's line with the text under it.

Playback errors use your app's language, with a tap-to-retry hint on phones and a
click-to-retry hint on desktop. On iPhone, playing a note after a call or dictation
uses normal playback audio rather than retaining the recording settings.

A report or a tracker you'll come back to can come as an [artifact](/docs/artifacts) instead: a page that opens from its card. Everything made or shared in a chat is also in its [Library](/docs/artifacts#the-library).

## Approval cards

When a bot's agent asks to run a command, change files or use another tool, an approval card appears in the chat, unless the bot's **Permissions** are **Approve automatically**. In the agent's Auto mode it asks only about what it won't decide on its own. It says what the agent wants ("Wants to run a command", "Wants to change files"…), the computer and folder it runs in, and **Details** with the command or the diff.

The answers come from the agent, usually:

| Answer | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Allow once** | Allow this request. |
| **Always allow** | Allow it and requests like it, as the agent defines that. |
| **Deny** | Refuse this request. |
| **Never** | Refuse it and requests like it, as the agent defines that. |

In the terminal UI, press `y` to allow once, `a` to always allow, `n` to reject, `N` to reject and say why, or `1`…`9` to pick an option.

The agent waits for your answer. If the turn ends before you answer, for example because you stopped it, the card reads **Expired — the agent moved on**.

With **Approve automatically**, no cards appear: Codync allows each request for you. Your agent's own permission rules still apply under any setting. To change the setting, see [Bots](/docs/bots#permissions).

Some agents keep working after their reply, for example a background helper that finishes a long job. With **Approve automatically**, what that work asks is allowed too, unless you stopped the bot's latest reply; under any other setting it's declined, since no card can wait for you. Once the bot is idle there's no **Stop**: to stop approving that work, switch the bot to **Ask me**. When that work is done, the bot can still send you its result as a message in its chat, unless you stopped its latest reply.

## Stopping a turn

- **Desktop and iPhone:** while the bot works and the box is empty, the send button turns into **Stop**.
- **Desktop menu bar or tray menu:** the bot's submenu has **Stop task**.
- **Terminal UI:** press Esc to leave typing, then `s`.

Stop ends the running turn and drops the messages still queued for it, which then read **Not sent — stopped**. The chat shows "Stopped." and no notification is sent.

## Replying to a message

You can reply to any of your messages or any message from the bot. The reply goes in the chat in time order and shows a quote of the message it answers at the top.

- **iPhone:** touch and hold the message, then **Reply**.
- **Desktop:** hover the message and click **Reply**, or right-click it.
- **Terminal UI:** press `r`, move to the message with `j` and `k`, then Enter or `r`. Esc cancels.

While you reply, the box shows the quote above it and reads "Reply…". The bot gets the quoted text with your reply, so it knows what you mean even when that message is from an earlier session.

## Reactions

- **iPhone:** touch and hold a message; the reactions are at the top of the menu.
- **Desktop:** hover a message and click **Add reaction**, or right-click it.
- **Terminal UI:** pick a message (`v`), then `1`…`6`.

The quick reactions are 👍 ❤️ 😂 🎉 👀 ✅. Tap your reaction again to take it back. In a bot's own chat, the bot isn't told about your reactions. Bots react now and then too, and their reactions show with their avatars.

## Finding text in a chat

On the desktop, press ⌘F (Ctrl+F on Windows and Linux) to search the open chat. Matches are highlighted as you type and the bar shows which one you're on. Enter or ⌘G goes to the next match, Shift+Enter or ⇧⌘G to the previous one, and Esc closes the bar.

On the iPhone and Android, tap the magnifying glass at the top right of a chat. The bar shows how many matches there are and which one you're on; the arrows (or the keyboard's search key) move between them, and the message holding the current match is highlighted. Close the bar with ✕.

In the terminal UI, press `/` or Ctrl+F in a chat. Type to search, Enter or ↓ goes to the next match, ↑ to the previous one, and Esc closes the bar.

Find searches the messages the chat has loaded, so scroll up (or use **Load earlier messages** on the desktop) to reach older ones.

Searching your bots moved to ⌘K (Ctrl+K). ⌘F still opens it when no chat is open.

## Commands

Type `/` at the start of a message to see the bot's commands:

- `/fast`: turns [Fast mode](/docs/agents#models-and-options) on or off, when the bot's agent offers it.
- `/servers` (terminal UI): opens the bot's [dev servers](/docs/dev-servers), while it has some.
- `/` followed by a skill's ID: tells the bot to read that skill first and follow it for this message. The bot's skills are listed after the built-in commands.

Any other `/command` goes to the agent unchanged, so the agent's own commands keep working. In the terminal UI that includes `/servers` while the bot has no dev servers. There's no command to start a new session: Codync starts one on its own when a conversation needs it, for a Claude Code or Codex bot whose [Summarize long chats](/docs/bots#summarize-long-chats) isn't Auto. See [Bots](/docs/bots#sessions).

## Fresh conversations

When a conversation in a bot's own chat has grown long, Codync may start the agent over with a summary instead of carrying all of it. The chat then shows a card saying why: the conversation is long and has gone quiet, you've been away a while after several summaries, or summarizing no longer frees enough room. Your next message starts the fresh conversation.

**What it carries** unfolds to what the new conversation begins with: the latest summary of the conversation, your last four messages word for word, the bot's last reply and its open plan. The bot can still search the earlier chat. The chat, memory, settings and files stay as they are.

Before your next message, you can:

- **Edit what it carries** (the pencil): change the text in **Edit the handoff** and save.
- **Keep this conversation:** the bot carries on in the same conversation.
- **Terminal UI:** pick the card (`v`), then `o` to show what it carries, `e` to edit it, or `K` to keep the conversation.

Afterwards the card folds into one line that unfolds to what was carried.

Fresh conversations come only from a Claude Code or Codex bot whose [Summarize long chats](/docs/bots#summarize-long-chats) setting is something other than Auto, the default. Changing it takes back a card the new setting no longer calls for, and the conversation carries on.

## Notifications

Your iPhone gets a notification when a bot:

| Kind | When | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Response needed** | It's waiting for you, such as on an approval card. | **Review request** |
| **Task complete** | It finished. Sent once, after its last queued message is answered. The text is an excerpt of its final reply. | **Open conversation** |
| **Task failed** | It hit an error or stopped short. | **Open conversation** |

You don't get one when:

- the Codync app on your iPhone is open and connected to the computer,
- the bot's **Notifications** switch is off, or the bot is hidden,
- you stopped the turn yourself.

Routine runs notify you too. A group chat sends one "done" once its bots have finished answering your message.

If system notification permission is off, **Push notifications** in **Manage computers** shows **Turn on notifications in Settings** instead of a switch. Tap it to allow notifications, or open iOS Settings if you previously denied permission. Returning to Codync refreshes the status in both Settings and Computer management. Your saved preference for that computer stays unchanged.

To turn notifications off on the iPhone, tap the connection status at the top of the Bots tab (such as **1 connected**), choose **Manage computers**, and switch off **Push notifications** on the computer whose alerts you don't want; each computer has its own switch. Previews, sounds and Focus follow your iOS settings. The push relay and Apple see only a generic line ("Your task is complete. Open Codync to read the result.") plus routing IDs. The bot's name and the message text are encrypted to your iPhone's key and decrypted on the phone. See [Privacy](/docs/privacy).

### Live Activities

When you send a message from the iPhone, a Live Activity for that bot appears on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. It shows whether the bot is working, needs you, is done or failed, without the message text. On the Lock Screen, a progress line of four segments in the state's color fills as the task moves: one while your message is sent or queued, two while the bot works, three when it needs you, four once it ends. A [voice call](/docs/voice#start-a-call) with the bot shows on its Live Activity too, with what the bot is doing while you talk. Turn it off in the **State** tab under **Live Activity**; that also keeps calls off it. The **Live Activity** and **Dynamic Island** settings control the same switch: the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island turn on and off together.

## Usage limits

Codync reads plan limits on your computer, without starting a conversation or model turn. It shows the agents your bots run. The button beside the computer's name opens every agent that can report, each with a switch and a handle, so you can turn on one no bot uses, hide one, or change their order: [which agents, and how](/docs/agents#usage-limits). To see them:

- **iPhone and Android:** the **State** tab, then **Usage limits**. There's also a Usage widget.
- **Desktop:** the menu bar or tray menu, or **Usage** in the account menu at the bottom of the sidebar.
- **Terminal UI:** press `U`, then `s` to choose and arrange.

Each limit reads as the share already used, such as **62% used**. Widgets and the small meters in the island and the terminal UI's status bar show just the number, which is also the share used.

## From a script

`codync-host send` messages a bot from a terminal or a script on the computer the host runs on:

```sh
codync-host send --bot Reviewer "Review the current diff without changing files."
codync-host send --bot Reviewer --wait --json --file request.txt
git diff | codync-host send --bot Reviewer --wait --file -
```

- `--bot` takes the bot's name (any letter case) or its ID. When two bots share a name, use the ID.
- The message is the words after the options, or a UTF-8 file with `--file`. `--file -` reads it from the input.
- Without `--wait`, the command prints the message's `id`, `seq` and `turn` once the host has it. With `--wait`, it waits until the bot is done and prints its reply. A reply in several messages comes joined. A turn that ends with no text, such as only a reaction, prints nothing. Group chats can't be waited on.
- `--json` prints one line of JSON instead: `botId`, `entryId`, `entrySeq`, `turn`, `status` (`completed`, `failed` or `timeout`), `stopReason`, `reply`, `replyId`, `notices`, `elapsedSeconds` and `waitedForApprovalSeconds`.
- `--reply-to <id>` quotes a message, like [Reply](#replying-to-a-message): pass the `entryId` or `replyId` of an earlier `--json` result.
- `--timeout <seconds>` (30 minutes by default) is how long `--wait` waits. Time waiting for an [approval](#approval-cards) doesn't count, and the command never approves anything. When the time runs out, the bot keeps working.

Notices and approvals the bot waits for go to the error output. The exit code says how it went:

| Code | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Done (without `--wait`: the host took the message) |
| 1 | The turn was stopped, hit a limit, was declined or failed |
| 2 | Wrong options, no bot or more than one with that name, or `--wait` on a group chat |
| 3 | The host isn't running or refused the message |
| 4 | `--wait` ran out of time |
