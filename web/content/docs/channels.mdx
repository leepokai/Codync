Talk to your bots from WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack or Discord. Only the people you allow can write to a bot there, and the bot answers with its own agent, folder and permissions.

## Connect a channel

Channels belong to a computer and are set up on it. Open **Extensions → Channels** on the desktop or iPhone app, pick the channel and use its page:

- **WhatsApp:** link a number with the QR code, choose the bot that answers it, and let chats or people in. A separate number is safer: WhatsApp can block one it sees as automated.
- **Telegram:** add the bot @BotFather gave you, choose the Codync bot it answers for, then send `/start` with its pairing code from Telegram.
- **Slack:** connect the workspace's app with its bot token, app token and signing secret; each channel is bound to a Codync bot and to the Slack users allowed in it.
- **Discord:** connect the server's bot token, then bind channel ids to Codync bots and list the people allowed in each.

The same controls remain in **Settings → General → Channels**, with a shortcut to Extensions. Each channel's page shows its state, the bot that answers, the people allowed, its memory and its picture.

Tokens stay in the computer's credential vault. A paired phone can see the channels, pair or remove someone, and change what a channel remembers.

## Channel memory

Each Telegram bot, Slack channel and Discord channel has a **Channel memory**: what the bot should know about that conversation, such as the project it covers or how its people like answers. It is given to the bot before every message from that channel and saved on its own, so it survives a reconnect.

A channel also wears a **Channel picture**: one of twenty icons or, the first choice, the faces of its members. It shows beside the channel in Settings.

Keep it short and stable. Anything that changes per message belongs in the message; anything that belongs to every chat belongs in the bot's [memory](/docs/memory).

## Approvals and routines

An [approval card](/docs/conversations#approval-cards) arrives in the channel with a button per option; anyone allowed there may answer, and the first answer wins. Mentioning the bot in a Slack or Telegram channel can also start a [routine](/docs/routines); those triggers are edited on the computer.
